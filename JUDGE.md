# Start here

**Crowdtape** — [live board](https://sunilswain7.github.io/crowdtape/) ·
[@crowdtape_bot](https://t.me/crowdtape_bot) · Markets and Trading Tools

## Sixty seconds

1. **Open the [board](https://sunilswain7.github.io/crowdtape/).** The first column is
   where each asset sits on CoinMarketCap's most-visited list. Underneath it is the
   market-cap rank, and the multiple between them.

   The most-looked-up asset on CoinMarketCap is routinely something ranked in the hundreds
   or thousands by size. At the time of writing it was **EDEL, looked up #1, ranked #682**,
   and **MALA at #4 with a market-cap rank of 4,183** — 1,046× more attention than size,
   up 58% in a day. Neither is visible in a top-200 universe, which is why the universe
   here is the union of the market-cap listing and the attention list.

2. **Scroll to the scorecard.** Every reading this thing has ever issued is graded against
   what the price actually did, on excess return over the market median. The verdicts are
   in plain words, and **one of our four readings has been rejected by its own record.**

3. **Ask the bot.** `/crowd` ranks everything by how looked-at it is against its size.
   `/score` gives you the same verdicts in a message. `/coin MALA` gives one asset in
   full — and tells you, unprompted, when the reading it just gave you has no measured
   edge.

## Why it is built this way

**Price is commodity data.** A dozen providers sell it. *What three hundred million people
looked up today* is CoinMarketCap's alone, and `trending/most-visited` is the only place
to get it. None of the other submissions we surveyed used that endpoint.

**CoinMarketCap does not keep it.** There is no `trending/historical`, no liquidations
history, no open-interest history. Everything interesting here is latest-only, generated
and discarded. So the first thing this project built was a recorder, and the dataset it
now holds — **thousands of snapshots at ten-minute spacing** — does not exist anywhere
else, including, as far as we can tell, at CoinMarketCap. The git history *is* the
dataset: every snapshot is a commit, timestamped by GitHub, independently verifiable.

**Nothing is judged against a fixed number.** Every axis is cross-sectional. The first
run against live data called 126 of 200 assets a signal, because the market was up 4.2%
that day and the test was "up more than 2%" — beta with a label on it. Price is now judged
on excess over the universe median, liquidation stress on rank within the universe.

## The part we would point at

**The scorecard says we are partly wrong, and it says so on the front page.**

`exit_liquidity` predicts underperformance. Its assets outperformed. We did not flip the
sign to fix the table — a few days of one strong uptrend is not enough to rewrite a
hypothesis, and in a bull tape "the crowd arrives late and it keeps going" is what
momentum does.

We also refuse to pool grades from two different models. Readings made while the crowd
axis was *turnover standing in for a crowd* are not graded alongside readings made on a
*measured count of people*. When the attention feed arrived partway through, pooling them
turned a rejected reading into an inconclusive one on arithmetic alone — so the scorecard
grades only the confident ones and shows both counts.

Every other submission asserts its signal works. This one measured, and published the
failure.

## Endpoints used

| Endpoint | What for |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/cryptocurrency/trending/most-visited` | the crowd axis — 24h, 7d and 30d |
| `GET /v1/cryptocurrency/listings/latest` | the market-cap universe, price, tags, contracts |
| `GET /v5/derivatives/liquidations/cryptocurrency/list/latest` | long/short liquidations at 1h, 4h, 24h |
| `GET /v5/exchange/derivatives/list` | open interest and derivative volume by venue |
| `GET /v1/dex/holders/count` | distinct wallets holding a token |
| `GET /v1/cryptocurrency/trending/latest` | corroborating attention |
| `GET /v1/global-metrics/quotes/latest` | total cap, dominance, derivative volume |
| `GET /v3/fear-and-greed/latest` | sentiment, for context |
| `GET /v1/key/info` | credit budget, and the only way to infer the plan |

## Where the API got in the way

[`FEEDBACK.md`](FEEDBACK.md) — eight findings, every one measured with its reproduction.
The short version: the two datasets CoinMarketCap uniquely has are the two it does not
retain; a path that does not exist answers **HTTP 200**; and naming any `aux` field
silently drops `platform`.

## Honest limitations

- **The attention feed was plan-gated for the first three days.** The hackathon's Startup
  upgrade reached this key on 21 September. Readings before that rest on turnover as a
  proxy, are marked unconfirmed everywhere they appear, and are excluded from grading.
- **The scorecard is young.** Grades on a measured crowd axis are in the tens, not the
  hundreds. The verdicts say "too early" where that is the truth.
- **Wallet counts cover ~138 of the universe** — the rest have no contract address
  CoinMarketCap's DEX index reaches.
- **All of it is one market regime.** A signal that works in an uptrend has not been
  tested.
- Not investment advice, and the board says so.

## Running it

```bash
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -t . -v   # 78 tests, no key, no network
CMC_API_KEY=... python3 scripts/probe.py        # what your key can actually reach
CMC_API_KEY=... python3 recorder/record.py      # one snapshot
python3 -m engine.report                        # rebuild the board from the snapshots
```

The board is static and holds no key. `engine/report.py` regenerates everything on it from
the committed snapshots, so every number is reproducible from this repository.
